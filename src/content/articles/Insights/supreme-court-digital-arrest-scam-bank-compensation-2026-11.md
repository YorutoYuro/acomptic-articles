Digital arrest scams and other forms of cyber fraud have raised serious concerns about the safety of customers' money and the responsibility of financial institutions in preventing fraudulent transactions.

The Supreme Court of India, through its order dated **29 October 2026** in *In Re: Victims of Digital Arrest Related to Forged Documents*, has emphasised that the regulatory measures proposed by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) must include provisions requiring financial institutions to compensate victims where a failure to exercise due diligence is established.

The Court has also highlighted preventive safeguards for banks, including temporarily suspending unusual transactions, automatically informing the nearest police station and physically verifying suspicious transactions.

In addition, WhatsApp LLC and other concerned digital intermediaries have been directed to submit details of the measures they propose to undertake to prevent digital arrest scams and related cybercrimes.

The directions form part of the ongoing proceedings in **Suo Motu Writ Petition (Criminal) No. 3 of 2025**.

Here is what the Supreme Court has directed and what the proposed measures could mean for banks, financial institutions, digital platforms and customers.

## What Has the Supreme Court Directed Regarding Bank Compensation?

One of the most important aspects of the Supreme Court's order concerns the responsibility of financial institutions when customers lose money through digital arrest scams and other cyber frauds.

The Court has reiterated that the regulatory measures proposed by the RBI must mandate suitable provisions requiring financial institutions to indemnify affected customers and pay compensation of **not less than the amount lost by the customer**.

However, this requirement is subject to an important condition.

The compensation provision applies where it is established that the fraud or crime occurred because the financial institution failed to exercise due diligence in preventing or reporting the incident.

This means that the proposed framework is intended to address situations where an institutional failure contributed to the customer's financial loss.

### When Will Banks Be Required to Compensate Victims?

The Supreme Court has specifically linked the proposed compensation requirement to the failure of a financial institution to exercise due diligence.

For example, suppose a customer normally carries out transactions of relatively small amounts, but a substantially unusual transaction is initiated from the account.

If the transaction is suspicious in light of the account's previous transaction history and the financial institution fails to take appropriate preventive or reporting action, the circumstances may become relevant when examining whether the institution failed to exercise due diligence.

If such a failure is established and the fraud occurred because of it, the proposed regulatory framework must provide for indemnification and compensation of not less than the customer's loss.

This is an illustrative example of the due diligence requirement and not an independent legal test prescribed by the order.

Importantly, the Supreme Court has not declared that every victim of a digital arrest scam is automatically entitled to compensation from the bank. The requirement discussed in the order is conditional upon an established failure of due diligence.

## What Preventive Safeguards Should Financial Institutions Follow?

Apart from compensation, the Supreme Court has emphasised the need for preventive safeguards that should be mandated for every financial institution.

The Court has identified measures intended to help detect suspicious transactions and prevent customers from losing money through fraudulent transfers.

These may include the following.

### 1. Temporary Suspension of Unusual or Suspicious Transactions

Financial institutions may be required to temporarily suspend transactions that appear unusual or suspicious when compared with the account's previous transaction history.

The objective is to identify transactions that differ significantly from the customer's normal banking behaviour and take preventive action before the fraud results in financial loss.

For example, if an account that generally carries out small transactions suddenly initiates an unusually large transfer, the transaction may require closer examination.

The proposed safeguard is intended to give financial institutions an opportunity to examine suspicious activity rather than allowing potentially fraudulent transactions to proceed without appropriate scrutiny.

### 2. Automatic Intimation to the Nearest Police Station

The Court has also highlighted the possibility of automatically informing the nearest police station about unusual or suspicious transactions.

This measure is intended to improve coordination between financial institutions and law enforcement agencies.

Timely reporting could help authorities become aware of potentially fraudulent activity and take appropriate steps.

### 3. Physical Verification of Reported Transactions

The Court has further referred to physically verifying reported transactions to establish whether they are genuine.

Under the proposed approach, after suspicious transactions are reported, the relevant verification may help determine whether the transaction was actually initiated by the customer or is connected with fraudulent activity.

These measures are intended to strengthen the preventive role of financial institutions in addressing cyber fraud.

## RBI's Proposed Know Your Customer Amendment Directions, 2026

The Supreme Court has decided to examine the **Reserve Bank of India (Know Your Customer) Amendment Directions, 2026** before issuing further appropriate directions in the matter.

The proposed regulatory directions are expected to follow this timeline:

- **Proposed notification date:** 30 November 2026.
- **Proposed operationalisation date:** 31 December 2026.

The Court considered it appropriate to examine the proposed RBI framework in light of the suggestions made by the learned Amicus Curiae and the directions proposed by the Union of India.

An important aspect of this examination is the inclusion of suitable provisions relating to financial institutions' accountability and compensation for victims of digital arrest scams and other cyber frauds.

### What Must the Proposed RBI Framework Address?

The Supreme Court has specifically emphasised that the proposed regulatory measures must incorporate provisions requiring financial institutions to:

- Indemnify victims where the required failure of due diligence is established.
- Pay suitable compensation of not less than the amount lost by the customer in such cases.
- Exercise appropriate due diligence in preventing suspicious transactions.
- Take suitable steps for reporting fraudulent or suspicious activity.

The Court has also highlighted preventive safeguards involving transaction suspension, police intimation and physical verification.

The final regulatory requirements will depend on the directions issued by the RBI and the subsequent proceedings before the Supreme Court.

## RBI Directed to Submit a List of Authorised Financial Institutions

To support the effective implementation of the proposed regulatory measures and the directions issued by the Court, the RBI has been directed to submit a list of all authorised financial institutions before the Supreme Court on the next date of hearing.

This direction is intended to assist the Court in examining the institutions covered by the proposed regulatory framework.

The RBI's submission will form part of the information considered in the ongoing proceedings.

## WhatsApp and Other Digital Intermediaries Must Propose Preventive Measures

The Supreme Court has also addressed the role of digital intermediaries in preventing digital arrest scams and related cybercrimes.

The Court has specifically directed **WhatsApp LLC (a Meta company) and other concerned intermediaries** to inform the Court about the measures they propose to undertake to prevent such scams.

They must also submit their proposals simultaneously to the **Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C)**.

This direction is important because digital arrest scams frequently involve fraudsters using digital communication platforms to contact individuals, impersonate government or law enforcement authorities and deceive them into transferring money.

The Court has therefore sought information about the preventive measures that digital intermediaries propose to undertake.

### What Are Digital Intermediaries Expected to Submit?

WhatsApp and other concerned intermediaries must:

- Apprise the Supreme Court of the measures they propose to undertake to prevent digital arrest scams.
- Address preventive measures relating to digital arrest scams and associated cybercrimes.
- Simultaneously submit their proposals to the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C).

The order requires these intermediaries to place their proposed measures before the Court. It does not prescribe a detailed list of specific technical measures in this particular order.

## Directions to State and Union Territory Law Enforcement Agencies

The Supreme Court has also directed law enforcement agencies across all States and Union Territories to provide information about digital arrest scam cases and the investigations undertaken.

The agencies must submit the following information:

1. **Status of Registered FIRs:** Details of FIRs registered so far in connection with digital arrest scam cases.
2. **Investigation Status:** Information regarding the investigations undertaken in those cases.
3. **Preventive Suggestions:** Appropriate suggestions for preventing, detecting and prosecuting such offences.

The Court has further directed the **Directors General of Police (DGPs)** of all States and Union Territories to ensure compliance with these directions.

### Information Must Also Be Forwarded to I4C

The Supreme Court has directed that the information furnished to the Court must simultaneously be forwarded to the **Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C)**.

This requirement is intended to ensure that the information submitted by State and Union Territory law enforcement agencies is also made available to the central cybercrime coordination mechanism.

The directions cover prevention, detection and prosecution, rather than limiting the reporting requirement to the number of complaints or FIRs registered.

## What Does the Supreme Court's Order Mean for Customers and Banks?

The Supreme Court's order highlights the importance of a stronger preventive framework for protecting customers against digital arrest scams and other cyber frauds.

For customers, the proposed safeguards could provide an additional layer of protection against suspicious transactions.

For banks and other financial institutions, the emphasis is on identifying unusual account activity, exercising due diligence and taking appropriate action when potentially fraudulent transactions are detected.

The proposed compensation provisions are particularly important because they address the financial responsibility of institutions where their failure to exercise due diligence is established.

However, customers should understand that the order does not create an unconditional right to recover every amount lost through cyber fraud.

The proposed compensation requirement is specifically connected to an established failure by the financial institution to exercise due diligence in preventing or reporting the incident.

The final regulatory framework will determine the detailed requirements applicable to financial institutions.

## What Should Banks and Financial Institutions Prepare For?

Although the proposed RBI Amendment Directions, 2026, are yet to be examined by the Court, the order highlights several areas that financial institutions should pay attention to.

The following points reflect the safeguards emphasised by the Supreme Court.

### 1. Review Transaction Monitoring Systems

Financial institutions should examine whether their existing systems can identify unusual transactions that are inconsistent with customers' previous transaction histories.

### 2. Examine Suspicious Transaction Handling

Banks should review the procedures followed when suspicious transactions are identified and consider the proposed safeguard of temporarily suspending such transactions.

### 3. Review Reporting Mechanisms

The proposed automatic intimation of suspicious transactions to the nearest police station highlights the importance of timely reporting and coordination with law enforcement agencies.

### 4. Examine Verification Procedures

Financial institutions should consider how suspicious transactions can be physically verified to establish their genuineness, as highlighted by the Court.

### 5. Review Due Diligence and Accountability

Banks and other financial institutions should pay particular attention to their due diligence procedures because the proposed regulatory framework must address compensation where an institutional failure is established.

These are practical areas arising from the Court's observations and should not be treated as a separate set of final regulatory requirements already brought into force by the order.

## What Happens Next in the Supreme Court Proceedings?

The Supreme Court has scheduled the matter for further consideration on **23 November 2026**.

Before the next hearing, the following submissions are required:

**Reserve Bank of India (RBI):**

- Submit a list of all authorised financial institutions before the Court.

**Law Enforcement Agencies:**

- Submit the status of FIRs registered in digital arrest scam cases.
- Provide details of the investigations undertaken.
- Suggest measures for prevention, detection and prosecution.
- Forward the information simultaneously to I4C.

**WhatsApp and Other Concerned Intermediaries:**

- Inform the Court about their proposed measures to prevent digital arrest scams and related cybercrimes.
- Submit their proposals simultaneously to I4C.

The Court will also examine the proposed RBI (Know Your Customer) Amendment Directions, 2026, which are expected to be notified by 30 November 2026 and operationalised by 31 December 2026.

The matter remains pending, and the next hearing will provide an opportunity for the Court to consider the information and proposals submitted by the concerned authorities and intermediaries.

## What Businesses and Customers Should Remember

The Supreme Court's order highlights several important developments concerning digital arrest scams, financial institution accountability and cybercrime prevention.

The key points are:

- The proposed RBI regulatory framework must provide for financial institutions to indemnify victims where a failure to exercise due diligence is established.
- Compensation in such cases should not be less than the amount lost by the customer.
- The Court has highlighted temporary suspension of unusual or suspicious transactions as a preventive safeguard.
- Suspicious transactions may be automatically reported to the nearest police station, followed by physical verification.
- The RBI has been directed to submit a list of authorised financial institutions.
- WhatsApp and other concerned intermediaries must submit their proposed preventive measures to the Court and I4C.
- Law enforcement agencies across States and Union Territories must provide information about FIRs, investigations and preventive measures.
- The next hearing is scheduled for 23 November 2026.

The main takeaway is that the Supreme Court is examining a coordinated approach involving financial institutions, digital intermediaries, regulators and law enforcement agencies to address digital arrest scams and other cyber frauds.

## Final Takeaway

The Supreme Court's order dated 29 October 2026 places significant emphasis on financial institution accountability and preventive measures against digital arrest scams.

The Court has reiterated that the RBI's proposed regulatory framework must include provisions for compensation of not less than the customer's loss where a financial institution's failure to exercise due diligence is established.

At the same time, the Court has highlighted transaction-monitoring safeguards, reporting of suspicious activity, physical verification and the responsibilities of digital intermediaries.

The directions to WhatsApp, other intermediaries, the RBI and law enforcement agencies also reflect the need for coordinated action against these offences.

With the matter scheduled for further consideration on 23 November 2026, the proposed regulatory measures and submissions from the concerned authorities will be important developments to follow.
