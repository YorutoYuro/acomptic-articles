# Supreme Court Calls for Bank Compensation to Digital Arrest Scam Victims, Directs WhatsApp to Propose Safeguards

The Supreme Court of India has reiterated that the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) must incorporate provisions requiring financial institutions to compensate victims of digital arrest scams and other cyber frauds where their failure to exercise due diligence is established. The compensation should not be less than the amount lost by the customer in such cases.

In an order dated **29 October 2026**, passed in *In Re: Victims of Digital Arrest Related to Forged Documents*, the Court also directed WhatsApp LLC and other concerned digital intermediaries to submit details of the measures they propose to undertake to prevent digital arrest scams and related cybercrimes.

The Court further directed the RBI to submit a list of all authorised financial institutions and asked law enforcement agencies across States and Union Territories to provide details of registered FIRs and investigations relating to digital arrest scams.

The matter is scheduled for further consideration on **23 November 2026**.

## Supreme Court's Directions on Bank Compensation for Cyber Fraud Victims

One of the important aspects of the Supreme Court's order concerns the responsibility of financial institutions in preventing digital arrest scams and other cyber frauds.

The Court specifically emphasised that the regulatory measures proposed by the RBI must contain suitable provisions requiring financial institutions to indemnify affected customers and pay compensation of not less than the amount lost.

However, this requirement is subject to an important condition: it must be established that the fraud or crime occurred because the financial institution failed to exercise due diligence in preventing or reporting the incident.

This means that the proposed compensation framework is linked to an established failure on the part of the financial institution. The order does not establish automatic compensation for every person who loses money in a cyber fraud.

### What Does This Mean for Banks?

Under the approach emphasised by the Supreme Court, financial institutions are expected to take appropriate preventive measures rather than merely respond after a fraudulent transaction has been completed.

Banks and other financial institutions may need to strengthen their transaction-monitoring systems, identify suspicious account activity and take timely action when unusual transactions indicate possible fraud.

The Court has also directed the RBI to examine these requirements while preparing its proposed regulatory framework.

## Preventive Safeguards Suggested by the Supreme Court

The Supreme Court referred to several preventive safeguards that should be mandated for financial institutions to reduce the risk of cyber fraud.

These measures may include:

1. **Temporary suspension of suspicious transactions:** Financial institutions may temporarily suspend unusual or suspicious transactions that are inconsistent with the customer's previous account transaction history.

2. **Automatic police intimation:** Suspicious transactions may be automatically reported to the nearest police station.

3. **Physical verification:** After reporting the suspicious transaction, the concerned financial institution may physically verify the transaction to establish whether it is genuine.

These safeguards are intended to help identify potentially fraudulent transactions before customers suffer significant financial losses.

The Court's observations highlight the importance of transaction monitoring and timely intervention, particularly where transactions differ substantially from an account holder's established banking patterns.

## RBI's Proposed KYC Amendment Directions, 2026

The Supreme Court has decided to examine the Reserve Bank of India (Know Your Customer) Amendment Directions, 2026, as part of its consideration of the measures proposed to prevent digital arrest scams and other cyber frauds.

According to the order, these amendment directions were proposed to be:

- **Notified by:** 30 November 2026.
- **Operationalised by:** 31 December 2026.

The Court considered it appropriate to examine the proposed regulatory framework before issuing further appropriate directions.

It also emphasised that the RBI's proposed measures must incorporate provisions relating to financial institutions' accountability and compensation for affected customers where the required failure of due diligence is established.

### RBI Directed to Submit List of Authorised Financial Institutions

To support the implementation of the proposed regulatory measures and the Court's directions, the RBI has been directed to submit a list of all authorised financial institutions before the Supreme Court on the next date of hearing.

This will assist the Court in considering the institutions covered by the proposed regulatory framework.

## WhatsApp and Other Intermediaries Asked to Propose Anti-Fraud Measures

The Supreme Court has also addressed the role of digital platforms in preventing digital arrest scams and related cybercrimes.

The Court specifically directed **WhatsApp LLC, a Meta company, and other concerned intermediaries** to inform the Court about the measures they propose to undertake to prevent such scams.

They have also been directed to submit their proposals simultaneously to the **Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C)**.

This direction brings digital intermediaries into the broader effort to address digital arrest scams, which frequently involve the use of digital communication platforms to contact and deceive victims.

The Court has sought preventive proposals rather than limiting its directions to action against fraudulent transactions after they occur.

## Directions to State and Union Territory Law Enforcement Agencies

The Supreme Court has also directed law enforcement agencies across all States and Union Territories to submit information concerning digital arrest scam cases.

The agencies must provide:

- The status of FIRs registered so far in connection with digital arrest scams.
- Details of the investigations undertaken in those cases.
- Suggestions for appropriate measures to prevent, detect and prosecute such offences.

The Court has further directed the **Directors General of Police (DGPs)** of all States and Union Territories to ensure compliance with these directions.

Additionally, the information furnished to the Supreme Court must simultaneously be forwarded to the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C).

These directions are intended to help the Court assess the progress of investigations and consider measures for improving the prevention and prosecution of digital arrest scams.

## What the Supreme Court's Order Means for Customers and Financial Institutions

The order highlights the need for a stronger preventive framework involving financial institutions, digital intermediaries and law enforcement agencies.

For customers, the proposed measures could provide an additional layer of protection against fraudulent transactions, particularly where suspicious activity is inconsistent with their normal banking behaviour.

For banks and other financial institutions, the Court's observations place emphasis on the importance of monitoring transactions, identifying unusual activity and exercising appropriate due diligence.

The proposed compensation framework is particularly significant because it addresses the financial consequences of cyber fraud where an institutional failure is established.

However, customers should understand that the order does not create an unconditional right to recover every amount lost through digital fraud. The compensation requirement discussed by the Court is specifically connected to the failure of a financial institution to exercise due diligence in preventing or reporting the incident.

The final regulatory requirements will depend on the RBI's proposed directions and further proceedings before the Supreme Court.

## What Happens Next?

The Supreme Court has scheduled the matter for further consideration on **23 November 2026**.

Before the next hearing:

- The RBI must submit a list of all authorised financial institutions.
- Law enforcement agencies in States and Union Territories must provide the status of FIRs, investigation details and suggestions for preventive and enforcement measures.
- WhatsApp and other concerned intermediaries must submit their proposed preventive measures to the Court and I4C.

The Court will also examine the proposed RBI (Know Your Customer) Amendment Directions, 2026, which are expected to be notified by 30 November 2026 and operationalised by 31 December 2026.

## Conclusion

The Supreme Court's order dated 29 October 2026 represents an important development in the ongoing efforts to address digital arrest scams and other cyber frauds in India.

By emphasising compensation where a financial institution's failure to exercise due diligence is established, the Court has highlighted the importance of institutional accountability in protecting customers from financial fraud.

Its directions to the RBI, law enforcement agencies, WhatsApp and other digital intermediaries also reflect a coordinated approach to preventing such offences.

The matter will be considered further on 23 November 2026, when the Court is expected to take up the compliance and regulatory measures placed before it.
