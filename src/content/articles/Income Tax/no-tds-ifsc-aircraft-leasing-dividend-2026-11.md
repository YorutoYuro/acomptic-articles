Companies operating in International Financial Services Centres (IFSCs) and engaged in aircraft leasing have received an important clarification regarding TDS on dividend payments.

The Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT), through **Notification No. 141/2026 dated 4 November 2026**, has prescribed a mechanism under which eligible dividend payments between qualifying IFSC aircraft-leasing units can be made without deduction of tax at source (TDS).

The notification has been issued under Section 400(1), read with Schedule VI [Table: Serial No. 11] of the Income-tax Act, 2025. It provides that tax shall not be deducted under Section 393(1) [Table: Serial No. 7] on specified dividend income paid by an IFSC unit primarily engaged in aircraft leasing to another qualifying company that is also an IFSC unit primarily engaged in aircraft leasing.

However, the benefit is subject to a prescribed compliance requirement. The recipient company must furnish a statement-cum-declaration in **Form No. 1(N)** to the paying company. The payer must also report the relevant payments in its prescribed TDS statement.

The notification is deemed to have come into force from 1 April 2026.

Here is how the new provision works and what eligible IFSC companies need to do.

## What Does CBDT Notification No. 141/2026 Provide?

The notification provides for non-deduction of TDS on specified dividend payments between qualifying aircraft-leasing units located in an IFSC.

Under the notified arrangement, both the paying entity and the receiving entity must meet the prescribed conditions.

The payer must be an IFSC unit primarily engaged in the business of leasing aircraft. The recipient, also referred to as the payee, must be a company that is an IFSC unit primarily engaged in the business of aircraft leasing.

The dividend income must also be eligible for exemption under Schedule VI [Table: Serial No. 11] of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

Where these conditions are satisfied and the prescribed declaration is furnished, the payer is not required to deduct TDS on the qualifying dividend payments made or credited after receiving the declaration.

In simple terms, eligible aircraft-leasing companies operating from IFSCs can receive specified dividend income without TDS being deducted at the payment stage, subject to the conditions in the notification.

## Who Can Claim the Benefit?

The benefit is specifically intended for qualifying IFSC units engaged primarily in aircraft leasing. It does not apply automatically to every company operating in an IFSC.

There are two parties involved in the transaction.

### 1. The Paying Company (Payer)

The payer is the IFSC unit making the dividend payment.

To fall within the notification, the payer must:

- Be a unit located in an International Financial Services Centre.
- Be primarily engaged in the business of leasing aircraft.
- Make a dividend payment to an eligible recipient company.

The payer is responsible for not deducting TDS on qualifying payments made or credited after receiving the prescribed statement-cum-declaration from the payee.

### 2. The Receiving Company (Payee)

The payee is the company receiving the dividend income.

To qualify under the notification, the payee must:

- Be a company that is a unit located in an IFSC.
- Be primarily engaged in the business of leasing aircraft.
- Earn dividend income eligible for exemption under Schedule VI [Table: Serial No. 11] of the Income-tax Act, 2025.
- Furnish the prescribed statement-cum-declaration in Form No. 1(N) to the payer.
- Complete the declaration and verification requirements for the relevant tax year.

Both parties must satisfy the applicable conditions. Merely having an IFSC registration or receiving dividend income is not sufficient to claim this particular TDS benefit.

## Form No. 1(N): The Declaration Required for Non-Deduction of TDS

One of the important aspects of Notification No. 141/2026 is the introduction of **Form No. 1(N)**.

The recipient company (payee) must furnish this statement-cum-declaration to the paying company (payer).

The purpose of the form is to confirm that the recipient is an eligible IFSC unit engaged in aircraft leasing and that its dividend income qualifies for exemption under the relevant provision of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

The form also identifies the tax year for which the eligible dividend income is payable.

### What Information Does Form No. 1(N) Require?

The prescribed form contains Part A, which requires the following information:

1. **Name:** Full name of the declarant or relevant entity, as required by the form.
2. **PAN:** Permanent Account Number.
3. **Name of the IFSC Unit:** Name of the unit located in the International Financial Services Centre.
4. **Address of the IFSC Unit:** Complete address of the unit.
5. **Contact Number:** Country code and telephone number.
6. **Email ID:** Email address of the relevant unit.
7. **Tax Year:** The relevant tax year in the YYYY-YY format.

After furnishing these particulars, the payee must provide the prescribed statement-cum-declaration and verification.

### What Does the Declaration Confirm?

The declarant must confirm that:

- The relevant unit is engaged in the business of leasing aircraft.
- Its dividend income is eligible for exemption under Schedule VI [Table: Serial No. 11] of the Income-tax Act, 2025.
- The unit continues to operate in an International Financial Services Centre during the relevant tax year.
- The unit continues to be engaged in the business of leasing aircraft during that tax year.

The declaration must also be verified by confirming that all the particulars furnished are correct and complete.

It must be signed by a person competent to sign the return of income under Section 265 of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

This makes the form an important compliance document. The recipient company is formally confirming its eligibility for the benefit rather than simply requesting the payer not to deduct TDS.

## When Can the Payer Stop Deducting TDS?

The notification also clearly specifies when the payer can stop deducting tax on eligible dividend payments.

Under the prescribed condition, the payer shall not deduct tax on payments made or credited to the recipient after the date on which it receives a copy of the statement-cum-declaration in Form No. 1(N).

This timing requirement is important.

The payer should first receive the prescribed declaration from the eligible payee. The non-deduction treatment under this notification applies to qualifying dividend payments made or credited after that date.

### Example

Suppose Company A and Company B are both qualifying IFSC units primarily engaged in aircraft leasing.

Company A declares a dividend payable to Company B.

If Company B furnishes the duly completed and verified Form No. 1(N) to Company A on 10 November 2026, Company A can apply the notified non-deduction treatment to qualifying dividend payments made or credited after receiving the declaration.

However, the payer should not assume that the notification automatically reverses TDS already deducted before receiving the declaration.

The important point is that receipt of Form No. 1(N) establishes the prescribed documentation requirement for subsequent qualifying payments.

## The Payer Must Still Report Dividend Payments in Its TDS Statement

The notification does not completely remove the payer's reporting responsibility.

Even though TDS is not required to be deducted on qualifying dividend payments covered by the notification, the payer must report the particulars of all such payments in its prescribed statement of deduction of tax.

This reporting requirement is provided under:

- Section 397(3)(b) of the Income-tax Act, 2025.
- Rule 219 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026.

Therefore, non-deduction of TDS does not mean that the payment can be ignored for reporting purposes.

The payer must maintain the relevant information and furnish the particulars of payments on which tax has not been deducted by relying on Notification No. 141/2026.

For companies handling multiple dividend payments, maintaining copies of the declarations and corresponding payment records will be important for compliance.

## How Are Aircraft, IFSC and Unit Defined?

The notification also clarifies which statutory definitions apply to certain terms used in it.

Rather than introducing separate definitions, it refers to existing legislation.

**Aircraft:** The term has the same meaning as assigned to it in Note 3 of Schedule VI of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

**International Financial Services Centre:** The term has the same meaning as assigned to it under Section 2(q) of the Special Economic Zones Act, 2005.

**Unit:** The term has the same meaning as assigned to it under Section 2(zc) of the Special Economic Zones Act, 2005.

These references are important when determining whether an entity and its activities fall within the scope of the notification.

Companies should check the relevant statutory definitions rather than assume that every business operating in an IFSC or dealing with aircraft automatically qualifies.

## From When Does Notification No. 141/2026 Apply?

Notification No. 141/2026 was issued on 4 November 2026.

However, its effective date is retrospective.

The notification specifically states that it shall be deemed to have come into force on **1 April 2026**.

This means its provisions apply from the beginning of the tax year commencing on that date, subject to the conditions and procedural requirements prescribed in the notification.

The explanatory memorandum also states that no person is adversely affected by giving the notification retrospective effect.

For eligible companies, this effective date is particularly relevant when reviewing dividend payments made during the period beginning 1 April 2026.

However, the payer's specific non-deduction obligation under the notification is linked to receiving the prescribed statement-cum-declaration. The retrospective effective date should not be interpreted as an automatic reversal of TDS already deducted on earlier payments.

## What Should Eligible IFSC Companies Do Now?

Companies involved in aircraft leasing through IFSC units should review their dividend payment arrangements and ensure that the new declaration and reporting requirements are followed.

Here are the important steps.

**For the receiving company (payee):**

- Confirm that the unit qualifies under the prescribed conditions.
- Check that the dividend income is eligible for exemption under Schedule VI [Table: Serial No. 11].
- Prepare Form No. 1(N) with the relevant tax year and required particulars.
- Ensure that the declaration confirms the unit's continued IFSC status and aircraft-leasing activity.
- Get the form verified and signed by a person competent to sign the return of income under Section 265.
- Furnish the completed declaration to the payer.

**For the paying company (payer):**

- Confirm that the recipient is an eligible IFSC aircraft-leasing company.
- Obtain a copy of the duly furnished and verified Form No. 1(N).
- Record the date on which the declaration is received.
- Apply the non-deduction treatment to qualifying dividend payments made or credited after receiving the declaration.
- Report all relevant payments in the prescribed TDS statement under Section 397(3)(b), read with Rule 219 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026.

Both parties should retain appropriate records to support the treatment adopted.

## What This Notification Means for the IFSC Aircraft-Leasing Sector

Notification No. 141/2026 provides an important procedural benefit for companies involved in aircraft leasing through IFSC units.

Where dividend income is already eligible for exemption under Schedule VI [Table: Serial No. 11] of the Income-tax Act, 2025, the notification provides a mechanism to ensure that qualifying payments can be made without TDS, subject to the prescribed declaration.

This can simplify dividend payment arrangements between eligible IFSC aircraft-leasing units and reduce situations where tax is deducted at the payment stage despite the recipient's eligibility for the underlying exemption.

At the same time, the notification maintains compliance safeguards through Form No. 1(N), verification by the recipient and reporting by the payer.

For businesses operating in this sector, the important task is to ensure that the declaration is properly completed and furnished before relying on the non-deduction provision.

## Final Takeaway

CBDT Notification No. 141/2026 introduces a specific TDS non-deduction mechanism for eligible dividend payments between IFSC units primarily engaged in aircraft leasing.

The key points to remember are:

- The benefit is limited to qualifying IFSC aircraft-leasing units.
- The dividend income must be eligible for exemption under Schedule VI [Table: Serial No. 11] of the Income-tax Act, 2025.
- The recipient company must furnish a statement-cum-declaration in Form No. 1(N) to the payer.
- The payer must not deduct TDS on qualifying payments made or credited after receiving the declaration.
- The payer must still report the relevant payments in its prescribed TDS statement.
- The notification is deemed effective from 1 April 2026.

The main takeaway is that eligible IFSC aircraft-leasing companies can now follow a prescribed process to receive qualifying dividend payments without TDS deduction. However, both the recipient's declaration and the payer's reporting obligations remain important.
